What is HY OAS?

OAS = option-adjusted spread. The yield premium investors demand to hold a junk bond instead of a risk-free Treasury of the same duration, after removing embedded call option value.

Higher OAS = more compensation demanded = less risk appetite.
Lower OAS = tighter conditions = more confidence in corporate health.
Below 300bp = late-cycle complacency historically. Above 600bp = recessionary stress. Direction and rate-of-change matter more than the level.

Why rate of change, not level?

The level tells you where credit is. The rate of change tells you where it is going and how fast.

5-day Δ ≥+30bp: primary alert trigger — investigate.
20-day Δ turning positive and accelerating: regime change, not a wobble.
380bp falling and 380bp rising are opposite regimes. Two identical levels can sit at opposite ends of the credit cycle.

Quality spread — CCC minus BB

The HY index blends BB (near-investment-grade) and CCC (near-default). When the gap widens, capital is discriminating — exiting the weakest names first.

A widening CCC−BB gap before the headline index moves is your earliest credit stress signal. Leads broad HY widening by days to weeks at cycle turns.
Gap widening while headline flat = early stress. Both legs falling, gap compressing = recovery phase.

OAS vs cuts — the regime diagnostic

The same number of priced cuts means opposite things depending on what credit is doing simultaneously.

Wide OAS + many cuts: forced/defensive easing. Something breaking.

Calm OAS + cuts: benign easing. Inflation normalising, no stress.

OAS rising + few cuts yet: credit leading rates — most actionable divergence.

Real rates — why subtract the breakeven?

Nominal rates include a real return plus expected inflation. Real rates determine the actual inflation-adjusted cost of borrowing.

Real = nominal − 5Y TIPS breakeven. 4% nominal with 3.5% inflation expectations = 0.5% real — far looser than 4% nominal with 2% expectations (2% real).
Positive real = restrictive. Negative real = accommodative even if nominal looks high. The zero line on the chart is the policy boundary.

STIR — short-term interest rate futures

STIR futures price the market's implied path for overnight rates at each future date.

SR3: 3-month compounded SOFR over the contract quarter.

ZQ: arithmetic average of daily EFFR over the contract month. Used for FOMC meeting-path math.

Convention: both quoted as 100 minus rate. Price 95.42 = 4.58% implied.
The day-weighting algorithm decomposes ZQ into pre- and post-meeting regimes to extract the market's FOMC expectation at each meeting.
